Q3Confidentiality and risk analysis
Risk governs which documents enter the system
Which risks arise, and how much should each one weigh?
RiskWeightWhat it actually decides
Confidentiality and reputation Which documents may leave the building. One serious breach ends the client relationship.
Regulatory and contractual Written client agreement before use, manual fallback on refusal, DPIA before indexing.
Incorrect findings Mandatory human approval and refusal without evidence. A citation is not a conclusion.
Cybersecurity Standard controls either way. Local adds patching and insider duties rather than removing them.
Vendor dependency Exportable documents and metadata, tested switching, annual contract review.
How much should risk weigh against the value at stake?
Fines are capped; accreditation loss is uncapped
Every excluded document costs us benefit
Local removes the provider, adds insider access
A weighted scorecard would fake precision
Exclude first, then compare the compliant options
Not one of these five risks selects cloud over local. They select what enters the system and who signs it off.

Q8Implementation strategy
A twelve-week pilot on approved content buys the evidence for every later decision
Which low-risk pilot, and what happens short, medium and long term?
Weeks 1 to 2
Baseline times measured, data classes sized in hours, ICT annex drafted and the accreditation body informed.
Gate Scope, named owners and a documented eligibility estimate agreed
Weeks 3 to 6
The pilot use case: internal procedures and synthetic cases. Five users and no real client file.
Gate Fifty validated questions, ten unanswerable, access tests passed
Weeks 7 to 12
After written client agreement, twenty representative document packages assessed by two auditors.
Gate At least 2.5 net hours per week, quality and cost confirmed
Months 4 to 6
Up to twenty users. The authorised audit RAG and the reporting templates go live, partner takes over support.
Gate Independent approval and monthly benefit tracking in place
Months 7 to 12
Model change and data export tested, contracts reviewed and growth loads simulated for forty users.
Gate Month twelve: cloud re-decided against on-premise on measured eligibility
If a gate fails, the manual workflow stays and the rollout stops. A failed pilot costs EUR 12,000 and answers the question.

+Additional consideration, RAG
Yes, build the audit RAG, because it makes every answer checkable
What is RAG, and how does it differ from uploading files to ChatGPT?
1 Approved base
Only documents we may legitimately reuse
2 Retrieve
Permissions enforced before results are picked
3 Answer
Only the retrieved passages reach the model
4 Cite
Source, page and version shown with every claim
5 Auditor approves
No evidence, no answer. Approval is recorded
The difference is not the algorithm. A chatbot upload also retrieves. Business RAG adds maintained sources, client separation, version control, deletion rules and a tested answer set.
Should it be part of the solution, and how do we build it?
Yes, from month four, once the pilot gates pass
Index inside the EU, protected like the originals
Model stays interchangeable after quality testing
What does RAG not solve?
Retrieved passages still leave the internal network
A correct citation is not a correct conclusion
It improves traceability and does not protect confidentiality
